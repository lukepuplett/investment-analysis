# CSCO — Financial Analysis (FY2026 Full Year / Q4)

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Period covered:** FY2026 (ended 7/25/2026), all four fiscal quarters
**Sources:** [2026_Q1_10q.htm](../quarterly/2026_Q1_10q.htm), [2026_Q2_10q.htm](../quarterly/2026_Q2_10q.htm), [2026_Q3_10q.htm](../quarterly/2026_Q3_10q.htm), [2026_Q4_press_release.htm](../quarterly/2026_Q4_press_release.htm) (unaudited; full FY2026 balance sheet/cash flow are in the press release even though no 10-Q is filed for Q4), [2026_Q4_earnings_call.txt](../quarterly/2026_Q4_earnings_call.txt)

## Executive Summary

**BLUF: Cisco closed FY2026 with record revenue of $63.3B (+12% YoY) and accelerating quarterly growth (Q1 +7.5% → Q4 +17.6%), driven almost entirely by Networking (+22% FY, now 55% of total revenue) on the back of AI infrastructure orders. Operating leverage was excellent — opex grew only 1.8% against 12% revenue growth, pushing GAAP operating margin from 20.8% to 24.3% — but gross margin compressed ~40bps YoY on hardware/memory cost mix, and the balance sheet shows a real working-capital cost of the AI ramp: inventories +80% YoY and total debt up ~$1.4B to fund it. Financial health is strong (investment-grade-quality margins, $15.9B cash, 99% of FCF returned to shareholders) but the current ratio has fallen to 0.93, worth monitoring as inventory build continues. Recommend: fundamentals justify continued coverage; valuation richness (see valuation analysis) is the offsetting consideration, not financial quality.**

**Networking is carrying the entire growth story.** It went from 52.2% of revenue in Q1 to 56.8% in Q4 and its YoY growth rate nearly doubled over the year (15% → 28%), consistent with management's "networking super cycle" narrative and the AI infrastructure order ramp (see [market analysis](2026_08_market_analysis.md) and [technical analysis](2026_08_technical_analysis.md)).

**Security's Q4 jump needs a caveat.** CFO Mark Patterson flagged on the earnings call that Q4's 14% security growth included "sizable on-prem [Splunk] deals that also had a little bit longer duration" — a one-time mix effect, not a clean run-rate. Full-year security growth was only +2%, and management guided to "high single digits" for FY2027, not a continuation of the Q4 spike.

## 3. Margins

| Metric | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | FY2026 | FY2025 | Trend |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|-----|--------|--------|-------|
| **GAAP Gross Margin** | 65.5% | 65.0% | 63.6% | 64.1% | 64.5% | 64.9% | ↘ **Compressing** (-40bps FY) |
| Non-GAAP Gross Margin | — | — | — | 66.3% | — | — | ↘ -210bps YoY in Q4 |
| **GAAP Operating Margin** | 22.6% | 24.6% | 25.0% | 24.7% | 24.3% | 20.8% | ↗ **+350bps FY — strong leverage** |
| Non-GAAP Operating Margin | — | — | — | 35.9% | 34.8% | 34.4% | ↗ +40bps FY |

**The two margin lines are telling different stories, and both are real.** Gross margin is compressing because the growth mix is shifting toward AI infrastructure hardware (Silicon One systems, optics) which management has explicitly said carries lower gross margin than the legacy portfolio — but they are willing to take it because it requires minimal incremental opex to capture (Chuck Robbins, earnings call: "our expenses to actually capture that growth are minimal in addition to what we spend today"). That shows up directly in the operating margin line: GAAP opex grew just **1.8%** FY2026 ($25,492M vs $25,030M) against **11.8%** revenue growth — the core source of the 350bp operating margin expansion. This is a genuine structural improvement, not a one-off.

**Watch item:** management guided FY2027 non-GAAP gross margin to 65–66% (Q1 guide), i.e., a further step down from FY2026's 66.3% Q4 exit rate, and confirmed on the call this reflects continued mix shift plus memory cost inflation, only partially offset by ~30 internal programs targeting memory-utilization efficiency and prior price increases (contributed ~4–5 points of revenue growth in FY2026, per CFO).

Net income growth (+30.3%) outpaced revenue growth (+11.8%) by roughly 2.6x, consistent with management's stated priority of "bottom line growing faster than the top line" — driven by the opex leverage above plus a large non-operating tailwind: FY2026 "Other income (loss), net" was **+$1,245M** vs **-$68M** in FY2025 (Q4 alone: +$822M vs +$53M), which the press release does not fully itemize. This swing contributed materially to the EPS growth premium over operating income growth and should be understood as partly non-recurring (investment gains, minority-stake marks) rather than pure operating performance — worth flagging in the [risk assessment](2026_08_risk_assessment.md).

**Inventory build is the clearest balance-sheet signature of the AI infrastructure ramp.** Inventories rose from $3.16B to $5.69B (+80%) over the fiscal year — consistent with management's commentary about securing supply directly with TSMC and building strategic inventory ahead of Silicon One/optics demand. This is a reasonable read given the order backlog ($9.3B in AI orders vs. ~$4B AI revenue recognized in FY2026), but it is a real use of working capital and the reason short-term debt nearly doubled.

**Current ratio below 1.0 is a structural feature of Cisco's balance sheet** (deferred revenue is a large current liability, $16.99B at Q4-end, reflecting subscription/services business, not a liquidity stress signal by itself) but the trend from 1.00 (FY2025) to 0.93 (FY2026), combined with the debt increase, is worth tracking quarterly rather than dismissing — flagged in the red flag audit in the [risk assessment](2026_08_risk_assessment.md).

**Operating cash flow was flat year-over-year despite 12% revenue growth and 30% net income growth** — the gap is explained by the working-capital consumption above (inventory build, receivables growth) plus a large increase in income taxes paid (net $2,304M outflow FY2026 vs. FY2025). Q2 FY2026's standalone OCF of just $1,822M (vs. $3,212M in Q1 and $3,757M in Q3) stands out and is worth watching in the Q2 FY2027 comparison to see if it's a recurring seasonal pattern or one-off.

**Capital return discipline remains intact** — 99% of FCF returned via dividends + buybacks, and the dividend was raised for the 15th consecutive year. $8.1B remains under the repurchase authorization.

## Key Financial Takeaways

1. **Revenue acceleration is real and Networking-concentrated.** Four consecutive quarters of YoY acceleration (7.5%→17.6%) is unusual for a company of Cisco's scale and is a genuine positive signal — but ~55% of revenue and effectively all of the growth premium is now riding on one segment tied to AI infrastructure capex cycles.
2. **Operating leverage is the standout number.** +350bps GAAP operating margin expansion on 1.8% opex growth is a structural improvement, not noise.
3. **Gross margin compression is a known, guided trade-off**, not a surprise — management is explicit that lower-margin AI infrastructure hardware is accretive to operating margin because of minimal incremental opex.
4. **The AI ramp has balance sheet cost:** +80% inventory growth and a mid-year debt increase are the working-capital price of chasing the order backlog; current ratio sliding to 0.93 bears watching.
5. **EPS growth (+31% GAAP) outpaced operating income growth (+31% GAAP operating income also, coincidentally close) partly due to a large non-operating income swing** (+$1,245M vs -$68M YoY) that is not fully itemized in the press release — needs the 10-K footnotes to assess recurrence.
6. **Q4/FY2026 figures are unaudited** (press release only); treat as provisional until the 10-K is filed (~early September 2026) and revisit this document at that point.
