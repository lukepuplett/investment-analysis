## Capital returns

- FY26: share buybacks of €3.1bn (two tranches of the existing up-to-€6bn programme). A third tranche of up to €2bn started 24 Sep 2026 and runs to 31 Mar 2027 at the latest.
- **Proposed FY26 dividend €2.30 per share** (FY25: €0.70), around 50% of net income attributable to shareholders adjusted for extraordinary non-cash effects. Policy is a 40–60% payout. AGM 25 Feb 2027. The presentation puts the FY27 cash dividend at about €1.9bn.
- **Additional buybacks of up to €9bn through the end of FY2030.** The framework is about €3bn of buybacks a year.
- FY27–30 capital allocation framework (management plan): about €60bn of sources (about €52bn operating cash flow after tax plus about €8bn opening net cash). Uses: about 45% shareholder returns ("more than €25bn by FY30"), about 25% operations and organic growth (including about €2bn of capex a year on average), about 30% "strategic value creation" (portfolio development, the Siemens Energy India Limited commitment, and keeping an investment-grade profile).

## Outlook and targets (management guidance, not facts)

**FY27 outlook (group):** comparable revenue growth 14–16%; profit margin before SI 14–16% (FY26: 12.4%), *including* the brand licence fee; net income about €5bn; FCF pre tax about €10bn. It excludes charges for future legal and regulatory matters. Other FY27 items from the presentation: special items about −€0.7bn ("mainly expenses related to Omterra rebranding as well as the carve-out of Transformation of Industry"); P&L tax rate about 27%; non-controlling interests about €0.4bn; financial result about +€0.5bn; capex about 5–6% of revenue; cash tax about €1.3bn.

- FY30 group revenue above €70bn. Profit before SI about 3x from FY26 to FY30 (Gas Services about 3x, Grid about 2.5x, Siemens Gamesa about €1bn by FY30). Double-digit EPS growth through FY30. The FY30 margins **exclude** the brand licence fee: the Siemens brand licence ends as the company becomes Omterra (announced 14 Jul 2026, rollout from 2027), and any remaining costs are to be reported as special items.
- The FY27 margin range of 14–16% was, at the 2025 CMD, a target for FY28. Siemens Gamesa's previous mid-term target was 3–5%; it now targets 8–10% by FY30 and cash-positive operations by FY28.
- Versus the 4 Nov consensus: FY27 consensus was revenue €50.3bn, profit before SI €7.41bn (14.7%), **net income €5.53bn** and basic EPS €6.23. The margin guidance brackets consensus, but **the ~€5bn net income guide is below it**. The presentation attributes this mainly to the ~€0.7bn of special items and a higher tax rate.

## Demand, backlog and capacity (company statements)

- About **85% of FY27 revenue is already covered by backlog**. The service backlog is about €71bn. **About €15bn of the backlog (around 9%) is data-centre related** (Gas Services and Grid).
- Backlog margin uplift FY22–26 (project-level margin): Gas Services new units +26 pp, GS service +2 pp, Grid +12 pp, Transformation of Industry +4 pp, Siemens Gamesa +1 pp. Gas Services new-unit backlog margin improved 14 pp in FY26 alone. About 39 GW of SRAs at year-end are expected to convert to firm orders in FY27, "with better margin profile vs. FY26".
- Gas market: the company puts annual gas additions at 140–150 GW on average over FY27–30 (including steam turbines in combined cycle) and global OEM capacity at about 130 GW. It reports a market share of about 30% (#1 in EMEA, #2 in Americas and APAC). The pre-close call said the market is supply-constrained, with no delays or cancellations of SRAs or orders and no data-centre project cancellations.
- Capacity: gas turbine, power transformer and GIS capacity to rise more than 50% from FY26 to FY30. Grid is investing about €4bn in its factory network over FY25–30, including a new North American factory. The pre-close call gave about 30 GW of annual gas turbine delivery capacity by 2030; medium gas turbines went from about 50 to about 80 units a year in FY26, and large ones from 35 to 50 units from FY27. 96% of capex projects were executed within budget in FY26.
- Outlook narrative: data-centre build-out "is likely to lead to further investment in power generation capacity and grid infrastructure, especially in North America." The pre-close call called data centres an additional driver, "not the foundation" of the outlook.
- Transformation of Industry is to be separated: carve-out, bring in a co-investor, keep a meaningful minority stake. The pre-close call said no final decision is expected before about mid-calendar 2027.
