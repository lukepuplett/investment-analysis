**Date:** 11 November 2026, after the FY2026 / Q4 FY26 results (preliminary, unaudited)

## Executive Summary

**BLUF: Siemens Energy is a cleaner way to own the physical bottleneck in electricity than businesses that rent compute on borrowed money. It makes the gas turbines, large power transformers, switchgear and HVDC links that are in short supply, and it is paid in advance to do so. FY2026 showed that scarcity turning into profit and cash. Profit before special items was €5,486m (12.4% margin, from 6.0%), net income €4,129m and FCF pre tax €8,799m. The backlog reached €164bn, and project-level backlog margins are up 26 pp (Gas new units) and 12 pp (Grid) since FY22. Management now guides 14–16% margins for FY27 and targets 20–22% for FY30. The thesis is confirmed and stronger than in May, with two qualifications. Q4 firm gas new-unit orders were only about 2 GW, with no large turbines and no reservation conversions, so FY27 depends on about 39 GW of slot reservations converting. And customer advances added €5.8bn of cash in FY26 but slowed every quarter, so the ~€10bn FY27 FCF guide needs orders to stay strong. Siemens Gamesa reached break-even on profit but still used €1.6bn of cash and has a shrinking backlog. At about €132.76 (11 Nov close), the shares are about 29% below the May price while earnings rose.**

## Pillar 1 – Electricity demand is outrunning the rate at which it can be built (Confidence: 9/10)

**Thesis:** electricity demand is rising from electrification, cooling, industry, replacement of ageing grids and, on top of that, data centres. It is growing faster than turbines, transformers and grid connections can be manufactured, permitted and commissioned. The scarce input is physical equipment and delivery slots, not demand.

**Evidence (FY26):**
- Orders of €68.1bn (+18.8% comp.) and a book-to-bill of 1.54. Backlog rose €26bn to €164bn, about 3.7x revenue, and about 85% of FY27 revenue is already covered.
- The company's own framing (pre-close call, 30 Sep 2026): "the principal constraint is not underlying demand. It is the pace at which physical infrastructure can be permitted, financed, built, connected and commissioned."
- Gas market: the company puts average annual gas additions at 140–150 GW over FY27–30 against roughly 130 GW of global OEM capacity (company estimates).

**Data centres are an additional driver, not the foundation.** About €15bn (around 9%) of the €164bn backlog is data-centre related (Gas and Grid). Management calls data centres "an important additional driver, but … not the foundation of our outlook." This makes the thesis less dependent on any single AI capex cycle than a pure AI-infrastructure name. It also means ENR.DE is a diluted AI proxy.

**Shift of orders towards the U.S.:** the U.S. was 41% of FY26 orders (€27.7bn, +70.8% comp.) and 27% of revenue. Europe/C.I.S./Middle East/Africa orders fell 9.1% comp. for the year and 24.9% in Q4. U.S. policy (tariffs, the bulk-power-equipment executive order) and U.S. project financing are now larger swing factors than before.

**Risk if wrong:** demand normalises, or industry capacity catches up, before the backlog is repriced. The backlog protects FY27–28; the exposure is beyond that.

## Pillar 2 – Pricing power on scarce equipment (Confidence: 8/10 for Gas and Grid; Gamesa is the counter-example)

The repo's framework puts the Layer-2 test plainly: the thesis "depends on whether scarce turbines and grid equipment give Siemens Energy the pricing power to make its backlog more profitable than past contracts."

**Evidence that it does (Gas Services, Grid Technologies):**
- Project-level backlog margin uplift FY22–26: Gas new units +26 pp (+14 pp in FY26 alone), Gas service +2 pp, Grid +12 pp, Transformation of Industry +4 pp, Siemens Gamesa +1 pp.
- **New orders above backlog:** management says orders booked now carry better margins than both the executed backlog and orders signed in the first half of FY26. The ~39 GW of SRAs expected to convert in FY27 carry a "better margin profile vs. FY26."
- Reported margins before SI: Gas 13.0% → 16.4% (FY25 → FY26); Grid 15.8% → 18.9%, with Q4 at 20.1%. FY27 guidance is Gas 18–20% and Grid 21–23%.
- Gas Services and Grid together made 88% of segment profit before SI in FY26.

**Limits on that pricing power:**
- **Grid pricing in Europe has levelled off.** The pre-close call said pricing in regulated markets such as Europe has "stabilized at a high level"; U.S. pricing is still at a premium. Further Grid margin gains "will increasingly be driven by backlog conversion, cost management, capacity utilization and operational excellence rather than further broad-based price increases." From here, Grid's margin story is about execution.
- **Capacity race.** Siemens Energy plans more than 50% more gas turbine, power transformer and GIS capacity by FY30, and others are expanding too. The company says industry additions "continue to be orderly." This is the main long-run risk to the 25–27% Gas and 24–26% Grid FY30 targets.

**The standing counter-example – Siemens Gamesa:** wind is a real energy need that has not produced returns. FY26 was break-even (profit before SI €8m, 0.1% margin), with two profitable quarters. But FCF pre tax was −€1,580m, the backlog fell to €31bn from €36bn (FY book-to-bill 0.55), and FY27 revenue is guided down 1–3%. The company says offshore orders slipped into FY27 and that European offshore build-out is below a sustainable level. Targets: 0–2% margin in FY27, 8–10% and about €1bn of profit by FY30, cash-positive operations by FY28. Treat these as unproven until the cash line turns.

## Pillar 3 – Cash generation, judged by quality (Confidence: 7/10)

**Thesis:** the business converts profit to cash well, and customer advances fund working capital. But headline FCF currently runs well above earnings because of advances, and that gap is order-dependent.

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | FY26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FCF pre tax | 2,869 | 1,975 | 2,319 | 1,636 | 8,799 |
| Cash-flow line: change in contract liabilities (advances incl. reservation fees) | 2,533 | 1,945 | 881 | 453 | 5,812 |
| Net income | — | — | — | 1,360 | 4,129 |

- FY26 operating cash flow was €9,958m. The contract-liabilities line contributed +€5,812m; other working capital absorbed cash (contract assets −€1,288m, inventories −€1,399m, receivables −€1,032m, payables +€358m). Net operating working capital inflow was about €2.45bn, so FCF pre tax before working capital was roughly €6.3bn, against profit before SI of €5.5bn. Underlying conversion is healthy.
- **The advance inflow decelerated every quarter**, from €2.5bn in Q1 to €0.45bn in Q4, as orders moderated.
- **FCF > net income is order-dependent.** The FY27 guide is FCF pre tax of about €10bn against net income of about €5bn. If order intake normalises, contract liabilities can stop growing or unwind, and FCF would then run below earnings for a period.
- Gas Services (−€2.8bn) and Grid (−€1.3bn) run on negative net capital employed. That is a strength while orders grow and a reversal risk when they shrink.
- Capex was 4.5% of revenue in FY26 (€1,970m). It is guided to about 5–6% in FY27 for the capacity build, then falling, to below 4% after FY28 (company plan).

**Monitor:** FCF pre tax next to the contract-liabilities line and net income every quarter.

## Pillar 4 – Balance sheet and capital allocation (Confidence: 8/10)

- **Net cash:** adjusted net cash was €7.8bn at 30 Sep 2026 (FY25: €4.8bn) after €3.0bn of buybacks and a €0.6bn dividend in FY26. Ratings shown by the company: BBB+ (stable) and Baa1 (positive).
- **FY27–30 framework (management plan):** about €60bn of sources (about €52bn operating cash flow after tax plus about €8bn net cash). About **45% goes to shareholders** ("more than €25bn by FY30"), about 25% to operations and organic growth (about €2bn of capex a year on average), and about 30% to strategic value creation (portfolio development, the Siemens Energy India Limited commitment, an investment-grade profile).
- **Dividend:** policy of a 40–60% payout. Proposed FY26 dividend €2.30 per share (FY25: €0.70), around 50% of adjusted attributable net income; AGM 25 Feb 2027.
- **Buybacks:** €3.1bn repurchased in FY26 under the up-to-€6bn programme. A third tranche of up to €2bn runs to 31 Mar 2027 at the latest, and up to €9bn more is planned through FY2030 (about €3bn a year).
- **Watch:** the 30% "strategic" bucket is the discretionary part. M&A could compete with returns.

## Pillar 5 – Competitive position (Confidence: 6/10)

- Gas turbines: about 30% global share (#1 EMEA, #2 Americas and APAC, company/McCoy basis).
- Gas service: a growing installed base. The company estimates about 250 GW of fleet additions FY24–30 and more than €400m of service revenue per GW over 20 years; the group service backlog is about €71bn. LTSA revenue starts about 3–4 years after a unit leaves the factory.
- Grid: #1 positions claimed in grid solutions and products, with 37 factories at 26 locations and about €4bn of factory investment FY25–30.
- The moat is supply, scale and installed base, not unique technology. It holds while capacity is scarce. Durability past FY30 depends on industry capacity discipline.

## Portfolio and structural changes

- **Transformation of Industry (TI) carve-out:** legal and operational separation, bringing in a co-investor and keeping a meaningful minority stake; the company intends to deconsolidate it. No final decision is expected before about mid-calendar 2027. TI missed its FY26 revenue assumption (+3.4% vs 5–7%) and is guided to 0–2% growth in FY27. The separation sharpens the electricity story; its near-term P&L effect is special-item cost.
- **Omterra rebrand and brand fee:** Siemens Energy and Siemens Gamesa will become Omterra (announced 14 Jul 2026, rollout from 2027), as the time-limited Siemens brand licence ends. FY27 special items are guided at about −€0.7bn, "mainly expenses related to Omterra rebranding as well as the carve-out of Transformation of Industry." **FY27 margin guidance includes the brand licence fee; the FY30 margin targets exclude it**, so FY27 and FY30 margins are not like-for-like. Remaining brand costs are to be booked as special items. The fee amount is not stated in the official pack.

## The two main qualifications

1. **Order conversion (Gas Services).** Q4 FY26 firm new-unit orders were about 2 GW: 52 small and medium turbines, **no large turbines and no SRA conversions**. Meanwhile 14 GW of new SRAs were added and 6 GW shipped. Year-end commitments are 104 GW: a 65 GW backlog plus **39 GW of SRAs that the company expects to convert in FY27**. SRAs are fee-backed and the company reports no cancellations, but they are not orders. Grid's Q4 orders also fell 19.3% comp. (no HVDC award). If SRA conversion slips, both FY27 orders and the advance-driven cash flow weaken.
2. **Advance-dependent cash.** Advances added €5.8bn to FY26 operating cash flow and decelerated every quarter. The ~€10bn FY27 FCF guide is about twice guided net income and needs strong orders.

## Valuation context (no model, no target)

On the 11 Nov 2026 close of about €132.76, the shares are about 29% below the €185.84 used in May. Equity value is roughly €112bn on about 842m weighted Q4 shares (about €104bn after the €7.8bn of net cash), about 30x FY26 basic EPS of €4.44. Earnings rose sharply over the same period, so the multiple compressed. The May €220–250 target, its DCF and the "fairly valued at €185.84" conclusion are retired. A valuation refresh, and the standard revenue-scenario and valuation-sensitivity tables, were deliberately left for later.
