## Executive Summary

**BLUF: The year confirmed the core of the thesis and beat it on numbers. The May thesis treated pricing power on scarce equipment as a hope; the backlog-margin data now shows it, at least for gas and grid. Profit before special items more than doubled to €5.5bn (12.4% margin, above the 10–12% guide). FCF pre tax was €8.8bn against a CMD-2025 plan of €4–5bn. Backlog reached €164bn, and the company reports project-level backlog margins up 26 pp in gas new units and 12 pp in grid since FY22. It also brought forward its 14–16% margin target by a year (to FY27) and set FY30 at 20–22%. Capital returns reset upward: a €2.30 dividend and up to €9bn more buybacks to FY30. There are three qualifications. (1) Q4 Gas Services firm new-unit orders were only about 2 GW, with no large turbines and no SRA conversions; FY27 orders depend on 39 GW of reservations converting. (2) Cash is flattered by customer advances (+€5.8bn on the contract-liabilities line), and that inflow fell every quarter, from €2.5bn in Q1 to €0.45bn in Q4. (3) The FY27 net income guide (~€5bn) is below the pre-results consensus (€5.5bn) because of ~€0.7bn of rebrand and carve-out special items and a higher tax rate. Meanwhile the share price fell from about €186 in May to about €133 (11 Nov close), so the business got better while the stock got cheaper. This is a research update, not a recommendation.**

## Thesis pillar review

### Pillar 1 – Structural demand (May: 9/10) → **Confirmed; reframe it.**
Demand is real and broad: FY orders +18.8%, U.S. orders +70.8% (41% of group orders), backlog +€26bn. The May text framed this as an "energy transition / policy mandate" story (EU Green Deal, IRA, TAM of €880–1,170bn). The company's own framing, and the better one for this repo, is that electricity demand is outrunning the physical rate at which generation and grid can be built. From the pre-close call: "the principal constraint is not underlying demand. It is the pace at which physical infrastructure can be permitted, financed, built, connected and commissioned." That is the bottleneck thesis. Data centres are an *additional* driver (~€15bn of backlog), not the base. That makes the thesis more durable than a pure AI-capex bet, but it also means ENR.DE is a less concentrated AI proxy than the README suggests.

**Warning inside the pillar:** EMEA orders fell 9.1% comp. for the year and 24.9% in Q4. Growth is now heavily U.S.-led. U.S. policy (tariffs, the bulk-power-equipment executive order) and U.S. data-centre/IPP financing are therefore larger swing factors than the May set assumed.

### Pillar 2 – Contracted backlog (May: 8/10, delta said 9/10) → **Strengthened in size and quality; watch firm conversion.**
- €164bn backlog, 3.7x revenue, about €71bn of it service; 85% of FY27 revenue covered.
- Quality evidence the May set lacked: project-level backlog margins up 26 pp (Gas new units) and 12 pp (Grid) since FY22. FY27 SRAs carry a "better margin profile vs. FY26".
- **Qualification:** Q4 Gas Services took about 2 GW of firm new-unit orders, **none converted from SRAs, and no large gas turbines**, while 14 GW of new SRAs were added. Total commitments are 104 GW, but 39 GW of that is reservations the company "expects" to convert in FY27 (typically about six months, per the pre-close call). SRAs are protected by reservation fees, but they are not orders. **The FY27 test is whether ~39 GW converts on schedule.** Grid's Q4 orders fell 19.3% (no HVDC award; the next is expected early FY27).
- Siemens Gamesa backlog fell to €31bn from €36bn, with a FY book-to-bill of 0.55. Gamesa revenue is guided to *fall* 1–3% in FY27.

### Pillar 3 – Cash flow (May: 7/10) → **Strengthened in amount; qualified in quality.**
- €8.8bn pre-tax FCF against a ~€8bn guide and a €4–5bn CMD plan. After tax, €8.0bn. Capex is still only 4.5% of revenue.
- **Cash quality:** the contract-liabilities line (customer advances including reservation fees) contributed +€5.8bn of the €10.0bn operating cash flow. Net of other working-capital builds (contract assets, inventories, receivables), the net operating working-capital inflow was about €2.45bn, so FCF before working capital was about €6.3bn against €5.5bn of profit before SI. Underlying conversion is good. But the advance inflow slowed every quarter (€2.5bn → €1.9bn → €0.9bn → €0.45bn) as orders moderated. A ~€10bn FY27 pre-tax FCF guide on ~€5bn of net income needs more advances (i.e. strong FY27 orders and SRA conversion). If orders normalise, contract liabilities can unwind and FCF would fall below earnings for a while. Gas (−€2.8bn) and Grid (−€1.3bn) run on negative capital employed; that is a strength while orders grow and a reversal risk when they shrink.
- May's "Net debt/EBITDA ~1.5x" was wrong (the company was net cash at FY25), and so was "no announced buyback".

### Pillar 4 – Valuation (May: 6/10, delta 5/10) → **Improved by price, not by any new model.**
The May set called the stock "fairly valued" at €185.84 (≈42.9x forward P/E), with a €220–250 target. Today, at about €132.76, the company is worth roughly €112bn (≈€104bn after net cash). That is about 30x FY26 basic EPS, set against a company plan of double-digit EPS growth to FY30, ~€10bn pre-tax FCF in FY27 (≈10% of EV, pre tax and advance-assisted) and >€25bn of shareholder returns over FY27–30. Earnings estimates rose while the multiple compressed. **The €220–250 target and its DCF are stale.** They were built on a different earnings base (FY26 EPS consensus €4.15; FY28 revenue tree of €60–65bn, above the company's own path of about €56bn at a 12–14% CAGR) and should not be quoted until redone. Whether a refresh is warranted is for discussion; none was built here.

### Pillar 5 – Moat / position (May: 6/10) → **Modestly strengthened.**
The company reports about 30% gas turbine share (#1 EMEA, #2 Americas and APAC) and #1 in grid solutions and products. Its gas service base of about 250 GW of fleet additions over FY24–30 is worth >€400m per GW over 20 years in service revenue, by its estimate. The gas market is still supply-constrained (company view: 140–150 GW a year of demand against ~130 GW of OEM capacity), and capacity additions across the industry "continue to be orderly". The moat question is whether that discipline holds as everyone expands (Siemens Energy itself plans >50% more GT/transformer/GIS capacity by FY30). It does not bite in FY27–28 because of the backlog, but it is the main long-run risk to the 25–27% Gas margin target.

### The framework test (THINKING_FRAMEWORK.md, Layer 2)
The repo's framework said the thesis "depends on whether scarce turbines and grid equipment give Siemens Energy the pricing power to make its backlog more profitable than past contracts." **FY26 is the clearest evidence yet that they do, for Gas and Grid.** Backlog margins are sharply higher, Grid margins passed 20% in Q4, and management says new orders and SRAs price above the executed backlog. Gamesa is the counter-example the framework named: break-even after years of losses, with a shrinking backlog, in a market where value goes to customers. The pre-close call also says Grid pricing has "stabilized at a high level" in Europe. Future Grid margin gains are to come from execution and utilisation, not price. That is a fair warning against extrapolating FY26's price-driven expansion forever.

## Warnings and qualifications (from the official materials)

1. **Q4 firm Gas orders thin** (about 2 GW, 0 large units, 0 SRA conversions). This is management phasing, per the pre-close call, but it is a fact that FY27 orders must come from SRA conversion.
2. **Advance-payment inflow decelerating** each quarter, and the FY27 FCF guide (~€10bn) is well above net income (~€5bn).
3. **FY27 net income guide (~€5bn) below the pre-results consensus (€5.53bn).** Drivers: ~€0.7bn special items (Omterra rebrand, Transformation of Industry carve-out) and a ~27% tax rate. The operating margin guide (14–16%) brackets consensus (14.7%).
4. **Transformation of Industry missed its revenue assumption** (+3.4% vs 5–7%; Q4 −2.9%, partly Middle East disruption) and is guided to 0–2% growth. The carve-out removes it from the core story, but the near-term P&L impact is special-item cost.
5. **Siemens Gamesa** still burned €1.6bn of FCF in FY26. Revenue is guided down in FY27, offshore orders slipped into FY27, and cash-positive operations are targeted only by FY28.
6. **FY30 margin target excludes the brand licence fee** (FY27 includes it). The fee ends with the move to the Omterra brand, so this is a real saving, not just an accounting choice. But FY27 and FY30 margins are not like-for-like, and "any potential remaining costs" will go to special items.
7. **Concentration shift to the U.S.** (41% of orders) and EMEA orders falling.
8. **Capex stepping up** to ~5–6% of revenue in FY27 (from 4.5%) for the capacity build. The company says this falls after FY27 and normalises below 4% after FY28.
9. **Results are preliminary and unaudited.** The Annual Report 2026 and its risk report are not yet out. The outlook excludes future legal and regulatory charges.

## Recommended changes to the written thesis (draft, for discussion; not yet applied)

1. **Recast Pillar 1** from "energy transition / policy mandates / TAM" to "electricity demand outrunning the physical rate at which turbines, transformers and grid can be built". Keep data centres as an upside driver (~9% of backlog), not the base case. Drop the large TAM tables.
2. **Add an explicit pricing-power pillar** tied to the framework test, with backlog margin uplift, new-order vs backlog margin and Grid's shift from price to execution as KPIs. Treat Gamesa as the standing counter-example.
3. **Rewrite Pillar 3 around cash quality**: report FCF pre tax next to the contract-liabilities line and net income every quarter; state that FCF > NI is order-dependent.
4. **Replace the capital-allocation text** ("no announced buyback", "net debt/EBITDA ~1.5x") with the FY27–30 framework: about 45% to shareholders, 40–60% payout, ~€3bn a year in buybacks, ~€2bn a year capex, net cash and investment grade.
5. **Add new items**: TI separation, the Omterra rebrand and brand fee, FY30 targets (12–14% CAGR, 20–22% margin, Gamesa ~€1bn profit), and the U.S. concentration of orders.
6. **Retire or redo the price target** and position-sizing tables. The €220–250 PT, the €150 stop and the "fairly valued at €185.84" language belong to a different price and earnings base.
7. **Update the monitoring dashboard**: replace "Gross Margin <21%" and "Operating margin 8.1%" with margin before SI by segment; add SRA GW converted per quarter, the contract-liabilities CF line, U.S. share of orders and Gamesa FCF.
