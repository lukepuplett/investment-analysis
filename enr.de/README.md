## Investment Thesis Summary (November 2026)

Siemens Energy sells the physical equipment that electricity demand is waiting on: gas turbines, large power transformers, switchgear, HVDC and grid stabilisation. Demand is outrunning the rate at which that equipment can be built, connected and commissioned. Data centres add to that demand but are not its base (about €15bn, or roughly 9%, of the €164bn backlog). FY2026 showed the scarcity turning into pricing power for Gas Services and Grid Technologies. Profit before special items more than doubled to €5.5bn (12.4% margin), backlog margins are sharply higher than in FY22, and the company now targets 14–16% margins for FY27 and 20–22% for FY30. Siemens Gamesa reached break-even but remains the counter-example of a real energy need that has not yet produced returns.

Two qualifications matter most:
1. **Order conversion.** Q4 Gas Services booked only about 2 GW of firm new-unit orders, with no large turbines and no conversions from slot reservation agreements (SRAs). FY27 depends on about 39 GW of reservations converting.
2. **Cash quality.** Customer advances added €5.8bn to FY26 operating cash flow, and that inflow fell every quarter. The ~€10bn FY27 FCF guide therefore needs orders to stay strong.

Full argument: [analysis/2026_11_investment_thesis.md](analysis/2026_11_investment_thesis.md).

## Revenue Breakdown & Growth Drivers Analysis

### Jargon Glossary

- **Profit before special items (SI):** the company's headline operating profit measure. It is before financial result, income taxes, purchase-price amortisation, goodwill impairments and items management treats as non-operational.
- **Comparable growth:** growth excluding currency translation and portfolio effects.
- **Book-to-bill:** orders ÷ revenue. Above 1.0 means the backlog is growing.
- **SRA (slot reservation agreement):** a fee-backed reservation of a gas-turbine production slot. The company says SRAs typically convert to firm orders in about six months. An SRA is not yet an order.
- **New Units / Service:** equipment sales versus maintenance, spares, upgrades and long-term service agreements (LTSAs) on the installed fleet.
- **LTSA:** a long-term service agreement. Gas LTSAs average about 17 years, per the pre-close call.
- **HVDC:** high-voltage direct-current transmission (multi-year projects, e.g. offshore and interconnectors).
- **LPT / GIS:** large power transformers and gas-insulated switchgear, the key Grid products being expanded.
- **Contract liabilities:** customer advance payments (including reservation fees) received before delivery. Growth in this balance supports operating cash flow.
- **Backlog margin:** the project-level margin embedded in booked but undelivered orders.

- Group FY27 also guides net income of about €5bn (below the 4 Nov consensus of €5.53bn, mainly from ~€0.7bn of rebrand and carve-out special items and a ~27% tax rate) and FCF pre tax of about €10bn.
- The FY30 target is revenue above €70bn, with profit before SI about 3x FY26 (Gas ~3x, Grid ~2.5x, Gamesa ~€1bn) and double-digit EPS growth through FY30.
- **FY27 margins include the Siemens brand licence fee and FY30 margins exclude it**, so the two are not like-for-like. Remaining brand costs are to be reported as special items.
- About 85% of FY27 revenue is already covered by backlog.

## Capital Allocation (FY27–30 framework, management plan)

- Sources of about €60bn (about €52bn of operating cash flow after tax plus about €8bn of opening net cash).
- Uses: about **45% to shareholders** ("more than €25bn by FY30"), about 25% to operations and organic growth (including about €2bn of capex a year on average), and about 30% to strategic value creation (portfolio, the Siemens Energy India commitment, an investment-grade profile).
- Dividend policy is a 40–60% payout; the proposed FY26 dividend is €2.30 per share (AGM 25 Feb 2027).
- **Buybacks:** €3.1bn was repurchased in FY26 under the up-to-€6bn programme. A third tranche of up to €2bn runs to 31 Mar 2027 at the latest, and up to €9bn more is planned through FY2030 (about €3bn a year).
- The company has been **net cash** throughout (adjusted net cash €7.8bn at 30 Sep 2026). Ratings shown by the company: BBB+ (stable) and Baa1 (positive).
