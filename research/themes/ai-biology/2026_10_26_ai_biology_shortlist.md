## BLUF

- The Citrini framing (filed 26 Oct 2026) says AI speeds up molecule generation, but in-silico binding accuracy was about 49%, wet-lab data took about 30x longer than generation, and human trials cannot be compressed. If that is right, **the money flows to whoever produces biological data and runs trials, not to whoever generates molecules.**
- The tape already agrees in part. Over the last year the data-generation tools have re-rated hard (TXG +420%, TWST +310%, ILMN +169%), while the AI-native drug discovery names are mixed (RXRX -39%, SDGR +6%, ABCL +109%). Some of the "picks and shovels" upside is therefore **already priced in.** Be wary of chasing it.
- Where I'd start: **IQV** (trials are the bottleneck that cannot be compressed, and the stock is not a momentum chart), **ILMN** (sequencing as the data layer, though it has already re-rated) and **LLY** (a pharma with cash flow that can buy whatever AI and biology produce). RXRX is the purest test of the AI-maximalist case. Treat it as a small, cheap option, not a core holding.

## Scenario test

| Bucket | Citrini right (slow biology, data and trials are the bottleneck) | AI-maximalists right (in-silico accuracy jumps, fewer failures) |
|---|---|---|
| A Picks and shovels | **Best placed.** Demand for wet-lab and trial work rises as more AI-generated hypotheses need testing | Still fine: more programmes means more tool and trial demand. Risk: better prediction cuts screening volumes, and AI-enabled trials cut CRO hours |
| B AI-native | **Worst placed.** They remain biotechs that need the same trials, and the platform premium fades. RXRX is only partly hedged because it generates its own data | **Highest beta.** The first approved AI-derived drug would re-rate the group, but incumbents (LLY, Isomorphic) may capture most of the value |
| C Platform pharma | Steady: value comes from late-stage assets already in hand, and AI is a small bonus | They gain through lower R&D cost per approval, and they can buy the winners. Valuation is the limiting factor |

Statistical caveat: history says roughly 90% of drugs entering clinical trials fail. That is the widely cited industry figure and was not re-verified for this note. No AI-derived drug has yet reached approval to prove that the success rate changes. Until it does, bucket B is a bet on something that has not been shown.

## Top 3: where I'd start research

1. **IQV.** This is the most direct play on the bottleneck that cannot be compressed (trials), and at +27% it is not a momentum chart. Q3 earnings land tomorrow (27 Oct), so the bookings and backlog figures will give a fresh, measurable test.
2. **ILMN.** Sequencing is the data layer of the thesis. The stock has already re-rated, so the job is to check whether volume growth justifies the move, using the 29 Oct earnings, before adding exposure.
3. **LLY.** Cash flow from GLP-1s funds the AI and biology bets, with clear 6–18-month catalysts (Q3 on 29 Oct; retatrutide BLA in Q1 2027). It holds up in both scenarios; the main question is valuation.

Speculative sleeve, if any: RXRX as a small option on the AI-maximalist case. Monitor the REC-4881 FDA update in 2H26.

## Luke's view (2026-10-26)

Luke's note: I used to hold Illumina. It ran up high and then crashed. It just makes machines, and I suspect others can make the machines in the new world. It's distribution that matters for AI breakthroughs.

Implication for this list: treat ILMN as a commoditising hardware supplier (competing sequencers already exist, for example Element, Ultima, MGI/Complete Genomics, PacBio and Oxford Nanopore) rather than a top-3 start. Favour names that control access to patients, prescribers, trial sites or proprietary clinical data: IQV (trial sites and patient data), TEM (oncologist and clinical data network) and LLY (global commercial reach to sell whatever gets discovered). ILMN is demoted to watch only.
