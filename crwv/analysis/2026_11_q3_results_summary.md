## Business outlook (as of 10 November 2026)

- FY2026 revenue raised to $13.0–$13.3bn. Immediate prior range, set in August, was $12.4–$13.2bn. The older $12–$13bn range was the guide before that.
- FY2026 adjusted operating income raised to $1.275–$1.375bn, "a 26% increase at the midpoint compared with our prior guidance" (midpoint $1.325bn implies a prior midpoint of about $1.05bn, matching the August $960m–$1.15bn range).
- Still expect more than 1.85 GW of active power at year-end.
- FY2026 capital expenditures $37–$39bn, "raising the low end of our prior guidance range of $35 billion to $39 billion" because infrastructure is expected to be deployed sooner. The $31–$35bn range was the guide before August.
- Q4 2026 interest expense $1.05–$1.15bn.
- Exit-2026 annualized run-rate revenue (last month of the quarter × 12) raised to $20–$21bn, from $18.5–$19.5bn in August.

## Other official points from the release

- Revenue backlog about $132.5bn at 30 September 2026. Definition: remaining performance obligations, plus other amounts estimated to be recognised as revenue in future periods under committed customer contracts, in each case subject to delivery and availability of service.
- About $9bn of customer prepayments and deposits expected in the next three months.
- Added more than 200 MW of active power to over 1.7 GW (doubled since the start of the year); contracted power grew more than 900 MW to over 4.6 GW; international contracted power more than 1.3 GW, including India, Indonesia and Spain.
- About $4.2bn raised through an upsized offering of convertible senior notes.
- Inaugural at-the-market programme for up to 35 million Class A shares.
- Closed a $2.6bn DDTL 5.5 facility, the first facility designed for shorter-duration customer contracts.
