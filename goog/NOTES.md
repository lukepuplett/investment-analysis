## 2026-10-30 — Luke's bull list, "search is dead", and the agent-ads test

Luke's bull list (his words, condensed): strategically in great places (plural); owns the higher ground; many products benefit from this sustaining innovation; an ads network marketers understand; lots of data; vertically integrated tokens and TPUs; its own frontier lab; the best ROI in cloud; about 6.5% of SpaceX (which includes xAI as its "SI" segment) and about 12% of Anthropic; self-driving (Waymo); about 100M new web users a year on cheap Android phones.

Luke's bear premise: "Search is dead... It has to die and this is it. That's not a question of if, it's, can they get that ad spend from some agentic product like this. Can they give it away?" General answer-search goes, and the other tabs (Maps, Shopping, YouTube, local) still matter.

My view:
- The market isn't disputing the bull list. It's discounting it for negative FCF, about $200B capex and correlated AI selling. The payoffs (TPU revenue from 2027, the ANT IPO marking up the stake, Waymo, Cloud margins) mostly arrive in 2027 or later.
- Informational queries make little money, and the money is in commercial intent. Answers moving to agents hurts traffic more than revenue. The real risk is agents capturing the buying intent.
- Google is best placed to give an agent away: it has a free-product, ad-funded model, about 3B Android users and the cheapest tokens to serve (TPUs). The open question is the business model: sponsored answers or purchase commissions instead of a results page. These are higher value per action, but there are fewer slots and a trust cost.

What to watch each quarter:
1. Search and other revenue growth, and paid clicks. Two quarters of actual decline would turn the bear case from story into data.
2. Whether Gemini or AI Mode carries ads or takes commissions on agent purchases, and at what pricing relative to Search.
3. Free cash flow against the capex guide; TPU external revenue from 2027.
4. The ANT S-1/A price range, which marks the stake against its $128B carrying value.
