## Key Observations

### The Post-Earnings Selloff
Corning beat on both the top and bottom line (core sales +17% YoY to $4.74B, core EPS +30% YoY to $0.78, both above guidance), yet the stock fell from a July 27 close of $143.36 to $124.05 by July 29 (~-13.5% over two sessions). The stock had rallied +96% over the trailing year and was trading at a Forward P/E of ~80x and EV/EBITDA of ~56x heading into the print (see 6/30/2026 column above) — a valuation priced for continued acceleration. Management's Q3 guide (sales +16% YoY, Core EPS +28% YoY) implies **similar, not accelerating**, growth versus Q2, which one sell-side analyst (Wamsi Mohan, BofA) explicitly flagged on the call as an apparent point of deceleration. Management pushed back, saying the guide "is not intended to imply any deceleration" and that the growth rate is expected to accelerate from Q4 2026 onward (19% CAGR through Q4 2030) — but at this valuation, an in-line-not-better quarter was evidently not enough to sustain the multiple.

### Valuation Reset
Even after the drop, Trailing P/E (57x) and Forward P/E (39x) remain well above the broader market and above GLW's own 12/31/2025 levels (56x / 29x), suggesting the stock gave back much of the 2026 re-rating rather than falling below where it started the year.
