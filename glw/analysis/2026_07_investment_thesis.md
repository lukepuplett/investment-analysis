## Investment Thesis Overview

### Thesis Summary (updated)
The core thesis from January is unchanged in substance: Corning is the leading integrated optical infrastructure provider capturing secular AI datacenter buildout, executing well operationally, and expanding margins ahead of a public plan. What's new this quarter is (1) confirmation that the growth story extends beyond scale-out fiber into a new, named "Photonics" platform targeting $10B by 2030 (co-packaged/near-package optics inside the server box), and (2) a market-level lesson that this stock's multiple can compress violently even on a beat-and-raise quarter if the *pace* of growth doesn't visibly accelerate. The investment case is now as much about **entry price discipline** as it is about the underlying secular growth logic — both of which were separately true in January but have now been demonstrated in real time by a ~45% round-trip in the stock price (April $167 → June $219.58B implied peak → July $124).

## Core Investment Rationale

### 1. Structural Secular Tailwind: AI Infrastructure Buildout — Reinforced with New Photonics Detail
The January thesis identified Optical Communications' Enterprise growth (+61% YoY at the time) as the primary pillar. Q2 shows this accelerating further (+65% YoY), and — critically — management has now quantified *why* this should continue outgrowing raw GPU shipment growth: three specific technical drivers (cluster-size-driven third optical layer, bandwidth/SerDes cycle effects, and scale-up network optical penetration) combine to an estimated 1.3x-1.5x increase in optical content per GPU by 2028. See the Technical Analysis document for full detail. The new Photonics MAP ($10B by 2030, co-packaged/near-package optics) represents a genuine expansion of Corning's addressable footprint from "between the boxes" to "inside the box" — a bigger structural claim than existed in January's thesis.

**Durability assessment, updated:** Unchanged qualitatively (secular, not cyclical), but the Photonics platform introduces a new, less-proven leg of the thesis alongside the well-established fiber/connector business. Treat Photonics contribution as high-uncertainty upside rather than a base-case certainty until it shows revenue traction.

### 2. Springboard Plan: Targets Reiterated, Not Raised or Cut This Quarter
No new targets were issued this quarter (management explicitly declined to "re-upgrade" the plan again so soon after May), but nothing was walked back either. The plan remains: $20B annualized run-rate by end-2026 (tracking a quarter ahead), $30B by end-2028, $40B by end-2030, with a 19% sales CAGR from Q4 2026 to Q4 2030 and earnings growing faster than sales. Management's "high-confidence" (more conservative, risk-adjusted) version of the plan targets $27B by end-2028 and $35B by end-2030 — still implying the company **doubles in size** by 2030 even under the conservative case.

**Key validation this quarter:** H1 2026 net income attributable to Corning grew 48.6% YoY on 18.3% revenue growth — operating leverage is visibly showing up in the numbers, not just in guidance language.

**New caution flag:** GAAP operating margin (15.5%) still lags the core/non-GAAP figure management cites (20.9%) by a wide margin. This gap isn't new, but this is the first thesis document to explicitly quantify and flag it — investors relying solely on management's core metrics are seeing a more flattering picture than the GAAP financials support.

### 3. Competitive Moat: Integrated Supply Chain + Product Leadership — Unchanged, with Photonics as a New Extension
The January assessment (integrated fiber+connector+assembly supply chain, 500+ patents, 18-24 month replication lag for competitors) still holds; nothing this quarter contradicts it. The Photonics platform, if it succeeds, would extend this integration advantage one layer further into the AI compute stack (inside the server box, not just between servers) — a logical moat extension rather than a new, unrelated bet.

### 4. Financial Execution & Cash Generation — Strong, with a New Dilution Watchpoint
**Q2/H1 2026 Results (GAAP):**
- H1 revenue: $8,649M (+18.3% YoY)
- H1 net income attributable to Corning: $930M (+48.6% YoY)
- H1 free cash flow: $1,325M (vs. $343M H1 2025)
- Diluted EPS: $1.06 H1 2026 (+47.2% YoY)

**New watchpoint — share count growth:** Shares outstanding grew from ~808M (12/31/2025) to ~906M (6/30/2026), roughly +12% in six months, driven substantially by a $500M "proceeds from warrants" financing inflow. This is real per-share dilution that partially offsets the impressive aggregate earnings growth. It did not stop diluted EPS growth from being very strong this half (+47.2%), but it is a headwind worth tracking — if warrant-related issuance continues at this pace, it will progressively eat into per-share metrics even as the underlying business grows.

**Cash flow quality caveat:** H1 operating cash flow (+142% YoY) was meaningfully aided by a $709M positive swing in "customer deposits and government incentives" — a working-capital/prepayment timing benefit, not purely organic. Don't extrapolate the H1 OCF growth rate as a clean run-rate without adjusting for this.

### 5. Path to $30B Revenue by 2028 (Upgraded Target) — On Track So Far
**H1 2026 baseline:** $8.65B revenue (six months); full-year run-rate implied ~$17-18B+ if H2 continues at similar or accelerating pace, tracking toward the "$20B annualized run-rate by end-2026" target management reiterated.
**2028 target (upgraded, May 2026):** $30B annualized run-rate (up from January's $24B internal-plan figure).
**Assessment:** No new data this quarter to contradict this path; Optical Communications (+32% YoY) and specifically Enterprise (+65% YoY) are the segments doing the heavy lifting, consistent with the plan's own assumptions.

## Conclusion

Corning's Q2 2026 results and the market's reaction to them together tell a consistent story: the underlying business is executing well and, if anything, the addressable opportunity (via the new Photonics platform) has grown since January — but the stock had gotten ahead of itself on valuation, and the market corrected that sharply and quickly rather than gradually. At $124.05, the entry price is now more attractive than at any point since this repo's coverage began re-rating the stock upward, while the fundamental thesis pillars remain intact or strengthened. The main new risk to track is not a fundamentals risk but a **timing and disclosure** risk — how fast optical scale-up architectures get adopted, and whether Corning's guided "reacceleration from Q4 2026" actually shows up in the numbers on schedule.

**Investment Case Strength: STRONG** ✅ (reaffirmed, with improved entry price)
