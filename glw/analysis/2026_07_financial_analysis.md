# Corning Inc. (GLW) – Financial Analysis
**Date:** July 30, 2026 | **Fiscal Period:** Q2 2026 (Quarter/Six Months Ended 6/30/2026)

---

## Executive Summary

**BLUF: Corning delivered a strong Q2 2026 beat: GAAP net sales of $4,505M (+16.7% YoY), GAAP operating income $698M (+21.8% YoY, 15.5% margin), GAAP net income attributable to Corning $559M (+19.2% YoY), diluted EPS $0.64 (+18.5% YoY). On management's core (non-GAAP) basis, sales were $4.74B (+17% YoY) and EPS $0.78 (+30% YoY), both above guidance, with operating margin expanding 190bps to 20.9% and free cash flow of $1.42B for the quarter. H1 2026 net income attributable to Corning is up 48.6% YoY on 18.3% sales growth — clear operating leverage. Q3 guide of $4.9–5.0B sales (+16% YoY) and $0.85–0.89 core EPS (+28% YoY) implies continued, not accelerating, growth near-term, with management guiding to reacceleration from Q4 2026 onward.**

## Key Financial Metrics (GAAP, per 10-Q)

### Income Statement Performance — Q2 2026 (3 Months)
- **Net sales:** $4,505M (+16.7% YoY from $3,862M)
  - Gross margin: 36.1% (vs. 36.0% Q2 2025) — essentially flat despite mix shift, reflecting real cost growth alongside volume
  - Operating income: $698M (+21.8% YoY), operating margin 15.5% (+70bps YoY)
- **Net income attributable to Corning:** $559M (+19.2% YoY from $469M)
  - Net margin: 12.4% (vs. 12.1% Q2 2025)
- **Diluted EPS:** $0.64 (+18.5% YoY from $0.54)
- **Non-controlling interest deduction:** $50M (up from $31M) — growing NCI drag, likely tied to solar JV/polysilicon structure; worth tracking as it dilutes attributable earnings growth relative to consolidated net income growth (+21.8%)

### Income Statement Performance — H1 2026 (6 Months)
- **Net sales:** $8,649M (+18.3% YoY from $7,314M)
- **Operating income:** $1,337M (+31.3% YoY from $1,018M), operating margin 15.5% (+160bps YoY)
- **Net income attributable to Corning:** $930M (+48.6% YoY from $626M)
- **Diluted EPS:** $1.06 (+47.2% YoY from $0.72)
- H1 net income growth (+48.6%) significantly outpacing H1 revenue growth (+18.3%) — strong evidence of operating leverage building through the first half, more pronounced than the Q2-only comparison (+19.2%) because Q1 2026 had an even larger YoY margin expansion.

### GAAP vs. Core (Non-GAAP) Reconciliation Gap
Management's core Q2 2026 figures (sales $4.74B, EPS $0.78) run meaningfully ahead of GAAP (net sales $4,505M, diluted EPS $0.64) — a ~$235M sales gap and $0.14 EPS gap. Per the 10-Q income statement, GAAP figures net out hedge-related items (translated earnings contract gain of $90M) and other non-operating expense; management's core measure adjusts these out along with discrete tax items and restructuring/impairment charges. **This gap should be watched** — a widening GAAP-to-core spread over time could indicate increasing reliance on non-cash/hedge adjustments to hit narrative targets. For this quarter the gap is broadly consistent with prior quarters and not flagged as a new concern.

### Cash Flow Strength — H1 2026
- **Operating Cash Flow:** $2,079M (+142% YoY from $859M) — a dramatic swing, driven heavily by a $709M positive contribution from "customer deposits and government incentives" (vs. only $43M in H1 2025) and a smaller build in working capital drag than usual
- **Free Cash Flow (OCF − CapEx):** $1,325M (vs. $343M H1 2025), a ~3.9x increase
- **Capital Expenditures:** $754M (+46% YoY from $516M) — CapEx is scaling with the growth investment (Optical Communications capacity, per management commentary), consistent with the upgraded Springboard plan
- Caution: the OCF surge is partly a working-capital/customer-prepayment timing effect (customer deposits line), not purely organic margin-driven cash generation. Normalize for this when comparing to FY2025's full-year OCF run rate.

### Balance Sheet Health — as of 6/30/2026
- **Total Assets:** $32,956M (+6.4% vs. 12/31/2025's $30,976M)
- **Total Debt (current + long-term):** $8,424M (roughly flat vs. $8,434M at 12/31/2025) — despite $500M in new debt issuance and $691M in repayments in H1, net debt levels held steady
- **Cash and cash equivalents:** $2,504M (up sharply from $1,526M at 12/31/2025, +64%)
- **Total equity:** $13,127M (+6.7% vs. $12,307M at 12/31/2025)
- **Current ratio:** 1.81 (up from 1.59 at 12/31/2025) — liquidity position strengthened materially in H1
- **Debt-to-equity:** 0.64x (down from 0.69x) — modest deleveraging on an equity-growth basis
- **Shares outstanding (issued − treasury):** ~906M (up from ~808M at 12/31/2025) — a notable increase, largely explained by a $500M "proceeds from warrants" financing inflow in H1 2026 (warrant exercise/conversion), which is dilutive and should be tracked in per-share metric trend

## Profitability Analysis

### Margin Trend
| Period | Gross Margin | Operating Margin | Net Margin (attrib.) |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Q2 2025 | 36.0% | 14.8% | 12.1% |
| Q2 2026 | 36.1% | 15.5% | 12.4% |
| H1 2025 | 35.6% | 13.9% | 8.6% |
| H1 2026 | 36.5% | 15.5% | 10.7% |

- Gross margin expansion is modest (+10-90bps depending on period) — the bulk of operating margin gain is coming from operating expense leverage (SG&A and R&D growing slower than revenue) rather than gross margin/mix improvement at the GAAP level.
- Note: these GAAP margins run well below the core (non-GAAP) margins management cites (39.6% gross, 20.9% operating for Q2) — the core figures strip out amortization of purchased intangibles and other adjustments not reflected in this GAAP-only view. Treat GAAP margins as the conservative reference point.

## Key Concerns & Watchpoints

1. **GAAP/Core gap:** Continue reconciling core EPS ($0.78) to GAAP EPS ($0.64) each quarter; a widening gap warrants scrutiny of what's being adjusted out.
2. **Share count dilution:** ~98M net new shares in H1 2026 (largely warrant-related) is a real per-share earnings headwind not fully visible in aggregate net income growth — diluted EPS growth (+18.5% Q2, +47.2% H1) is already net of this, but worth tracking the pace going forward.
3. **OCF quality:** H1 operating cash flow benefited significantly from a $709M customer deposits/government incentives inflow — confirm this recurs or normalizes in H2 before extrapolating the OCF run-rate.
4. **Solar segment:** Still loss-making ($7M net loss in Q2) though management reiterates Q3 improvement; this is the fourth consecutive period flagged as "ramp headwind, improving next quarter" — monitor for actual inflection.
5. **Non-controlling interest growth:** NCI deduction growing faster (+61% YoY in Q2) than consolidated net income (+21.8%) — implies a growing minority-owned piece of the business (likely solar/polysilicon JV structure) capturing a rising share of growth before it reaches Corning shareholders.

**Longer-Term (Springboard Plan, reiterated from May 2026 Investor Day, unchanged):**
- Annualized sales run-rate targets: $20B by end-2026 (tracking a quarter ahead of plan per management), $30B by end-2028, $40B by end-2030
- Sales CAGR of 19% from Q4 2026 to Q4 2030, with earnings growing faster than sales
- New Photonics MAP (co-packaged/near-package optics) targeted at $10B revenue by 2030
- Operating margin: management continues to target "at or above 20%," core basis — will not be resetting the target this quarter, promised an update "later this year"
- ROIC: 14.9% currently (core), targeted to continue improving into "high teens" through the planning cycle

## Conclusion

Corning's Q2 2026 results confirm continued strong execution: double-digit revenue growth, expanding operating margins, and materially improved cash generation, with H1 net income growth (+48.6% YoY) well ahead of revenue growth (+18.3% YoY). The quality of the beat is real but not flawless — gross margin expansion at the GAAP level is modest, cash flow strength is partly aided by a working-capital/customer-deposit timing benefit, and diluted share count grew ~12% in six months on warrant proceeds. None of these are red flags individually, but together they explain why a headline beat-and-raise quarter still produced a sharp stock selloff (see Valuation Analysis) — the market appears to be pricing in the details, not just the headline growth rate.
