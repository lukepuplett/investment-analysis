**Quality of the FCF:** working capital contributed about **+$2.6B** to OCF in Q3 (payables and accruals +$3,991M, deferred revenue +$1,255M, against prepaid −$1,818M, receivables −$565M, inventory −$313M). Strip that out and OCF would be about $3.7B and FCF about −$1.1B, roughly the same as Q2. So the swing back to positive FCF is mostly timing, not a step-up in underlying cash generation. Capex is running at about 2× last year's rate (TTM $15.4B).

Balance sheet: PP&E up $3.3B QoQ to $50.6B; other non-current assets up $3.1B to $12.6B (includes the SpaceX stake). Debt is still almost all non-recourse ($9,265M; recourse $2M). Separately, an 8-K filed 2026-09-29 shows Tesla replaced its $5B Citi revolver with new Wells Fargo-agented facilities (including a $2.0B 364-day revolver and a term loan); nothing was drawn on the old revolver.

## SpaceX special-committee merger review

**Corrected 2026-10-23: the 10-Q does disclose it.** The 8-K deck and the October 8-K/proxy filings said nothing, but the 10-Q (filed 22 Oct), Part II Item 5 "Other Information", says:

> "The Tesla board of directors has formed a special committee (the "Special Committee") comprising disinterested directors to explore deepening Tesla's relationship with SpaceX, which could be through certain arrangements including without limitation a business combination (a "Transaction"). While there could be merits to a Transaction, there is no assurance that any such Transaction will be consummated, as a Transaction would be subject to, among other things, the affirmative recommendation of the Special Committee and, if agreed upon, any necessary shareholder approval."

So the Barclays (16 Oct) and ARK (21 Oct) merger talk in the diary is now backed by a company disclosure that a committee exists and is exploring options, including a combination. No terms, timetable, advisers or committee members are named. The 2025 compensation special committee (Denholm and Wilson-Thompson) in the proxy is a separate body. Next things to watch: any 8-K Item 1.01 or 8.01, and a proxy if a deal needs a shareholder vote.

## Against ROBOTAXI_ANALYSIS.md: does anything break the thesis?

**No. The thesis stands and is not rewritten.** Points against the quarterly checklist:

- *Cumulative safety miles (path to 5B by end-2028):* the doc doesn't say whether the 5B miles are supervised or unsupervised. Supervised FSD is already about 15B, but unsupervised is about 2M, three orders of magnitude short. At 8× QoQ growth it could close fast, but that rate won't hold. **Flag:** the metric definition should be pinned down in the next model refresh.
- *Intervention rate, remote-ops cost, Tier 3 approvals:* still not disclosed. No checklist item can be ticked from the filing.
- *Escalation triggers:* none hit. No fatality or incident was disclosed, and there's no evidence of a stall.
- *Core-auto context:* the doc's May valuation anchor ($398.87) predates this print. The core business looks steadier (auto GM ex-credits 18.1%), but operating margin is still compressed by R&D and SBC, and the positive FCF relies on working capital. Neither changes the robotaxi option math.

## 10-Q addendum (reconciled 2026-10-23)

- **Deck vs 10-Q:** Q3 revenue ($28,046M), segment revenue, cost of revenue, R&D ($2,761M) and gross profit ($5,494M) all tie. 9M OCF $14,897M and 9M capex $13,014M are consistent with the deck's quarterly figures. No corrections to the tables above.
- **SpaceX stake:** fair value **$2,865M** at 30 Sep (cost $2.00B). Q3 loss $142M, 9M gain $863M. In Q3 the regulatory restrictions expired, so the discount for lack of marketability was released and the stake moved from Level 2 to **Level 1** (quoted price). The shares stay under customary SpaceX IPO sale restrictions until **December 2026**. Tesla's stake is under 1%, accounted as equity-method with the fair-value option.
- **Timing of the $2.0B purchase:** the 10-Q says the investment was made in **March 2026** (cash flow line "Purchase of SpaceX equity investment (2,002)"). That confirms the FMP data-quality flag above: it belongs in Q1, not Q2.
- **SpaceX as a customer:** $221M Q3 / $626M 9M Megapack revenue from SpaceX (cost $171M / $478M). Receivable from SpaceX $729M and related deferred revenue $648M, mostly milestone billings for undelivered Megapacks.
- **New acquisition (Note 3):** in Q2 2026 Tesla bought a Super Intelligence (SI) hardware company in an asset deal for **$1.95B in stock and equity awards**. $1.73B of that depends on service conditions or deployment milestones (no expense booked yet, as the milestones are judged improbable), and $222M went to a patent/technology intangible. The company is not named.
- **Inventory:** $14,092M at 30 Sep vs $12,392M at 31 Dec 2025 (raw materials up $891M), even as finished-goods days of supply fell.
- **Credit facilities:** the 29 Sep five-year revolver, 364-day revolver and delayed-draw term loan agreements are filed as Exhibits 10.1 to 10.3.
- **Thesis impact:** the robotaxi thesis is unchanged. The special committee is a new, live corporate-structure question for a TSLA holder (a SpaceX combination would change what a Tesla share owns), and it's Luke's call how to weigh it.
