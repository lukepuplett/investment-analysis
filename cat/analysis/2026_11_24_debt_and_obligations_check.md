## Free cash flow: the two capex series

The earlier figure of about $8.6bn for nine months (operating cash flow $10.59bn less cash purchases of property and equipment $2.01bn) leaves out **$1.21bn spent on equipment leased to others** (2025: $1.02bn), which Caterpillar reports on a separate line. With both lines, nine-month free cash flow is about **$7.4bn**, not $8.6bn. Which series is right depends on the question; this repo uses both lines for the conservative figure.

## Reading it

The industrial business carries about $10.7bn of debt against $11.6bn of nine-month operating profit, which looks manageable. The finance arm's roughly $35bn is the larger number, but it is lending against receivables and leased equipment, so its risk is credit quality on the loan book and the cost of refinancing (about $8bn of its long-term debt falls due within a year), not the industrial company's solvency (inferred from the structure, not checked against the loan-loss disclosures). The loan book, allowance for credit losses and past-due rates are the items to read next.
